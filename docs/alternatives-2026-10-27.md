# No Tool Yet Matches math-board's Chalkboard

As of 27 October 2026 nothing online fits math-board's use case, and nothing comes close enough to adopt instead of building: across roughly 100 candidates checked against live sources, **no project meets more than four of the eight fit criteria**, and none meets criteria 4, 5 and 8 together (a science-grade teaching surface, a lesson timeline with camera and replay, and sandboxed view-only rendering). The nearest cousins each fail on a structural axis rather than a missing feature. **dsh-aha-tutor** reproduces the handwriting chalkboard, the camera that follows the teacher, pause/rewind, and even the same SVG allow-list and KaTeX `trust:false` posture, but it is a plugin for the DeepSeek Harness desktop app and an interactive course, not a pi or MCP companion ([README](https://github.com/BaronCyrus/dsh-aha-tutor/blob/main/README.en.md)). **bobpuley/agent-whiteboard** is the only MCP server that calls itself a whiteboard for AI teacher agents that humans do not edit, yet it returns no snapshot or layout feedback, has no writing animation, camera or infinite canvas, and is licensed PolyForm Noncommercial ([repo](https://github.com/bobpuley/agent-whiteboard)). The Excalidraw-based **whiteboard-mcp** and **mcp-excalidraw-server** give any terminal agent a loopback canvas with screenshots back, but they are human-editable diagram scratchpads with no LaTeX, plots, molecules or steps. **tldraw offline** names Pi as a supported agent but is closed source, drives the canvas through a raw-JavaScript exec port, and lets the human edit ([README](https://github.com/tldraw/tldraw-offline)). ByteDance's **Gauth Unlimited Digital Canvas** (launched 1 October 2026) is the UX twin, with chapters side by side, narration-synced writing and zoom from concept map to formula, inside a cloud subscription app with no API ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gauth-launches-unlimited-digital-canvas-gives-students-one-continuous-whiteboard-to-visualize-explore-and-understand-complex-topics-302891205.html)). The field has converged on three archetypes: an agent co-editing a human whiteboard, a voice-first cloud tutor bundling its own model keys, and an offline Manim or Remotion video generator. math-board's combination of a declarative validated scene, a step history the viewer can scrub, and a loopback view-only page belongs to none of them. Inside the pi ecosystem, the **5,990-package gallery returns zero hits for "blackboard"** and contains no teaching board of any kind ([pi.dev](https://pi.dev/packages?name=blackboard)). The practical yield of the survey is therefore ideas to borrow (a figure router that declines to draw, anchor-grid placement with an occupancy table, per-step layout checks, anchored viewer comments, a Playwright-free snapshot path, a `default-src 'none'` iframe for script-driven animation) rather than code to adopt.

## Three archetypes dominate, and none is a view-only lesson board

Every candidate the researchers found falls into one of three shapes, and the shape, not the feature list, is what disqualifies it. The first archetype is the **agent-bridged human whiteboard**: tldraw offline, the Excalidraw and draw.io MCP servers, Miro, FigJam, Whimsical and the dozen Excalidraw-based hobby servers all let an agent draw, but their value proposition is that the human draws back, so criterion 1 is violated by design and criterion 8 cannot hold because the page must stay editable. The second is the **voice-first cloud tutor**: HeyTutor/Accelute, SeeThrough, ChalkTalk, WhiteBoardAI, Gauth, VideoTutor and a dozen 2026 Product Hunt launches draw progressively while speaking, but each runs its own LLM loop with its own keys, so criteria 2 and 3 fail together and the board is unreachable from pi. The third is the **offline explainer generator**: TheoremExplainAgent, Code2Video, Math-To-Manim, Animo and the 2026 Manim-agent papers emit a finished video after a multi-minute batch pipeline, so criterion 5 (a mutable scene the viewer steps through) and criterion 8 (no model-written code execution) are structurally out of reach.

Four criteria are unmet by anyone in any category. **Criterion 5 as a whole**, meaning animated reveal plus chapters on an infinite canvas plus a camera that moves the viewer plus step back/forward/replay, has no counterpart: the closest are dsh-aha-tutor (pages on one drag/zoom canvas, view follows the teacher, pause/rewind) and Gauth (chapters side by side, zoomable), neither reachable from a terminal agent ([dsh-aha-tutor README](https://github.com/BaronCyrus/dsh-aha-tutor/blob/main/README.en.md); [Gauth](https://www.producthunt.com/p/gauth-ai-course/gauth-unlimited-digital-canvas)). **Criterion 4's science scope** is met by nobody: no candidate in any ecosystem renders molecules from SMILES or bundles a science-illustration library, and chemistry LaTeX appears only in WhiteBoardAI's "chemical equations" claim and in math-board itself ([WhiteBoardAI](https://github.com/stefeincurry/WhiteBoardAI)). **Criterion 6 as automated layout checks on a live board** is met only by alby13/AI-Whiteboard's bounding-box-plus-free-space map and, on files rather than a board, by DrawMe's structural linter for `.drawio` output ([AI-Whiteboard](https://github.com/alby13/AI-Whiteboard); [DrawMe](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@senad-d/drawme)). **Criterion 8 in full** is matched only by dsh-aha-tutor's SECURITY.md, which is the single other document found that enumerates KaTeX `trust:false`, an SVG allow-list that bans scripts, `foreignObject` and external `href`, and a no-network sandbox for animations ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/BaronCyrus/dsh-aha-tutor/blob/main/SECURITY.md)).

One infrastructure fact frames the whole MCP category. pi has no core MCP support; the community adapters `pi-mcp-adapter` (about 1.4M monthly downloads) and `pi-mcp-extension` (about 650K) bridge tool calls but render nothing, and no terminal host renders MCP Apps at all: a 2026-10-20 compatibility matrix lists Claude Code as "renders text only", and the Claude Code issue asking for MCP App rendering was closed as not planned on 2026-05-26 ([host matrix](https://www.mcpbundles.com/guides/mcp-apps-host-compatibility); [claude-code#48132](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/48132); [pi.dev](https://pi.dev/packages)). Any MCP board that relies on an in-chat iframe is therefore inert for pi; only servers that open their own browser tab (which is what math-board does) are even candidates.

## Agent canvases and whiteboard MCPs stop at co-edited diagram scratchpads

**tldraw offline** is the closest commercial neighbour and the only product in this group that names Pi. It is a free desktop app with "no accounts or servers" that "works without an internet connection", lists "Codex, Claude Code, Pi, and OpenCode" as supported agents, and was launched on 2026-07-16; it has 670 stars and was last pushed 2026-10-23 ([README](https://github.com/tldraw/tldraw-offline); [launch post](https://tldraw.dev/blog/tldraw-offline)). It fails criterion 3 because "tldraw offline is not open source. All rights reserved." It fails criterion 8 because its agent surface is a port that "could accept raw JavaScript and run it directly", documented by a third-party skill as `POST /api/doc/:id/exec` against the live editor with a bearer token, and because "Scripts stored in a `.tldraw` file can run when the file is opened" ([third-party skill doc](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/skills/optional/creative/creative-tldraw-offline); [README](https://github.com/tldraw/tldraw-offline)). It fails criterion 4 outright (tldraw shapes only; KaTeX is not importable in scripts, since "Only `tldraw`, `react`, and `react-dom` imports are supported") and criterion 1 (an editable whiteboard). It scores partial on 5 (SDK camera and frames exist, no step model), 6 (`api.getScreenshot` exists; lint only in a community MCP wrapper) and 7 (file save plus crash recovery). The companion **tldraw Agent Starter Kit** has the best agent context in the survey (viewport screenshot plus tiered BlurryShape/FocusedShape/PeripheralShapeCluster JSON plus lints) but runs its own model loop in a Cloudflare Worker, needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` or equivalent, and sits under an SDK license that forbids production use without a key, so it fails 2, 3, 4 and 8 ([starter kit](https://tldraw.dev/starter-kits/agent); [LICENSE](https://github.com/tldraw/tldraw/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). The hosted **tldraw MCP App** only renders in MCP-Apps hosts and saves to tldraw.com, and **tldraw computer** has no 2025–2026 status source at all ([tldraw blog](https://tldraw.dev/blog/tldraw-mcp-app)).

**mcp-excalidraw-server (yctimlin/mcp_excalidraw)** is the most popular open-source neighbour: 2,575 stars, MIT, npm 2.1.2 published 2026-10-04, "26 tools over stdio", a canvas server bound to "127.0.0.1 only by default", headless PNG via `screenshot` since v2.1, and skills for Claude Code and Codex ([repo](https://github.com/yctimlin/mcp_excalidraw); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/mcp-excalidraw-server)). It meets 2, 3 and 6 but fails 1 ("Opening the URL is still the way for a human to watch the agent draw", yet the tab is a full editor whose edits sync back), fails 4 (Excalidraw primitives plus Mermaid import; no LaTeX, plots or SMILES), fails 5 (viewport tools but no steps, animation or replay), and is partial on 7 ("By default the canvas is in-memory (restart = blank canvas)") and 8 (loopback, "no built-in authentication", fonts from esm.sh). **ansuman-shukla/whiteboard-mcp** is newer (created 2026-10-22, pushed 2026-10-27, 725 weekly downloads) and better hardened: a per-user token file with 0600 permissions, Host and Origin checks, bundled fonts so "the viewer makes no requests to the internet", `canvas_focus` that scrolls the human's view and pulses a highlight, and `canvas_screenshot` "so the agent can check its layout" ([repo](https://github.com/ansuman-shukla/whiteboard-mcp); [security.md](https://github.com/ansuman-shukla/whiteboard-mcp/blob/main/docs/security.md)). It still fails criterion 1 by design ("sees what you draw back"), criterion 4 (shapes, arrows, text, Mermaid; no LaTeX) and criterion 5 (no steps, animation or replay).

Three MCP servers are literally built for AI tutors, and each fails a different axis. **bobpuley/agent-whiteboard** states "Agent Whiteboard is not a canvas that humans edit directly", renders `katex`, `svg`, `html`, `mermaid` and `vega-lite`, builds step-frame sequences the learner steps through, blocks on `wait_done`, and snapshots every render to `~/.agent-whiteboard/<workspace>/`; it meets criterion 1 ([repo](https://github.com/bobpuley/agent-whiteboard)). But it fails 6 completely ("None returns an image of the board or reports overlaps or bounds"), each `render()` "Replaces whatever is currently on screen" so there is no canvas, camera or writing animation (partial 5), it has "no authentication on any endpoint" and no SVG sanitization beyond DOMPurify for HTML (partial 8), it is licensed PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 (partial 3), has 2 stars, and documents only Claude Code. **coldcat8120/interactive-mcp** is a "dedicated blackboard window" where "the LLM generates SVG; the frontend renders it as strokes appearing over time" with KaTeX, usable from any stdio MCP client ([repo](https://github.com/coldcat8120/interactive-mcp)). It fails 6 and 7 (no snapshot, no persistence), has no camera, chapters or replay (partial 5), and "Embeds KaTeX math via `foreignObject`", the exact element math-board's validator rejects, with no sanitization described (partial 8). **alby13/AI-Whiteboard** is the strongest on feedback: `look` returns a picture and `get_board` returns "Ids, labels and bounding boxes of everything, plus a map of free space", a pen "moves as it writes", and `save_board`/`load_board` are named ([repo](https://github.com/alby13/AI-Whiteboard)). It fails 4 (no LaTeX, plots, Mermaid or SMILES anywhere in the README), is partial on 1 ("You can draw too: pick up a marker"), partial on 5 (a fixed 1600×1000 board with no chapters or camera), has 0 stars and a single commit dated 2026-10-08.

The remaining open-source canvases are human-editable editors with an agent bridge. **draw.io MCP** (official, Apache-2.0, 5,720 stars, 14,048 weekly downloads of `@drawio/mcp`) opens diagrams in the editor and has a Claude Code plugin, but has no screenshot tool, no steps and no view-only mode, so it fails 5 and 8 ([repo](https://github.com/jgraph/drawio-mcp)). **kamiazya/whiteboard** (Apache-2.0, JSON Canvas files, "owner-only local socket and no network port", plugins for Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI) co-edits boxes and arrows and fails 1, 4 and 5 ([repo](https://github.com/kamiazya/whiteboard)). The official **Excalidraw MCP App** streams "with smooth viewport camera control and interactive fullscreen editing" but renders only in "Claude, ChatGPT, VS Code, Goose", none of them a terminal ([repo](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw-mcp)). **moki-whiteboard**, **openboard**, the proprietary **Whiteboard-MCP** binary and the $29 **whiteboard-mcp.com** are all editable and math-free ([moki](https://github.com/JKZeng-cell/moki-whiteboard); [openboard](https://github.com/atpaawej/openboard); [whiteboard-mcp.com](https://whiteboard-mcp.com/)). Every cloud canvas fails criterion 3: **Miro MCP** needs OAuth 2.1 and "No tool exports or screenshots a board" ([Miro tools](https://developers.miro.com/docs/miro-mcp-tools)); **FigJam** via Figma MCP is hosted and will "eventually become a usage-based paid feature" ([Figma help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/32132100833559-Guide-to-the-Figma-MCP-server)); **Whimsical** has a desktop MCP over localhost with `board_snapshot` but is a proprietary workspace app ([Whimsical tools](https://whimsical.com/learn/ai/mcp-tools)); **Eraser** requires paid API tokens ([Eraser docs](https://docs.eraser.io/using-ai-agent-integrations)); **Napkin** bills credits and has no MCP ([Napkin API](https://www.napkin.ai/api/)). The chat side panels (Claude Artifacts, ChatGPT Canvas, Gemini Canvas) fail 2 and 3; the one terminal-agent path among them, **Claude Code Artifacts**, is genuinely view-only and versioned with a strict CSP sandbox, but is "stored on Anthropic-operated infrastructure", requires a claude.ai login and is "not available with API keys" ([docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/artifacts)). The **Obsidian JSON Canvas MCP** writes `.canvas` files that Obsidian, an editor, displays, with no camera or screenshot, failing 1, 5, 6 and 8 ([repo](https://github.com/Cam10001110101/obsidian-jsoncanvas)).

## The pi gallery holds 5,990 packages and no teaching board

The pi ecosystem was searched exhaustively and returned nothing. The gallery shows "1-50 / 5990" packages; exact-name filters return **0 hits for "blackboard"**, 4 for "whiteboard", 6 for "canvas", 2 for "tutor" and 13 for "teach", and the top 50 by monthly downloads contain no visual or drawing package ([pi.dev](https://pi.dev/packages); [?name=blackboard](https://pi.dev/packages?name=blackboard)). The GitHub topics `pi-extension` (1,767 repositories) and `pi-package` (1,837) have no drawing or board project in their top 20 by stars; the one repo named pi-blackboard is a key-value store for parallel agents ([pi-extension topic](https://github.com/topics/pi-extension); [pi-package topic](https://github.com/topics/pi-package)). pi's core extension API offers tool renderers, dialogs, widgets and custom terminal components but "no browser-opening, HTTP or HTML/SVG preview API", and its roughly 105 example extensions include nothing for a browser, canvas, image, SVG, Mermaid or LaTeX ([extensions.md](https://github.com/earendil-works/pi/blob/main/packages/coding-agent/docs/extensions.md)). What exists instead falls into five families, none a board.

The **generic visual surfaces** are the most mature. **pi-generative-ui** (1,167 stars, MIT, pushed 2026-06-03) ports Claude's `show_widget` to native Glimpse WKWebView windows, one per widget; it meets 1 and 2 but fails 5, 6, 7 and 8 because "Widgets are display-only from the agent's perspective — there's no return channel", libraries load "from CDN", there is no persistence and the window "has full browser capabilities" ([repo](https://github.com/Michaelliv/pi-generative-ui)). **@nicknisi/pi-artifacts** (1.6.0, 2026-10-20) serves HTML from "a per-process HTTP server bound to `127.0.0.1`" with SSE live reload, an isolated-origin frame and comments sent back to the agent, but "supplied HTML are trusted local extension code", there is no model-facing snapshot, and no lesson model ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@nicknisi/pi-artifacts)). **sideshow** (628 stars) is "A live visual surface for terminal coding agents" with Mermaid, HTML and image cards and a pi extension plus MCP, but it is cards with comments, not steps, and its bind address, auth and storage are undocumented ([repo](https://github.com/modem-dev/sideshow)). **pi-canvas** (0 stars, 2026-09-12) is the nearest pi-native shape: "Binds strictly to `127.0.0.1`", pushes Mermaid 11, SVG and sandboxed HTML over SSE, and the human cannot edit; it meets 1, 2 and 3 but fails 5, 6 and 7 outright and does not state SVG sanitization ([repo](https://github.com/lleontor705/pi-canvas)). **pi-markdown-preview** (2,130 weekly downloads, the most-downloaded math-capable pi viewer) mirrors the transcript with MathJax and Mermaid loaded from jsDelivr; the agent places nothing ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/pi-markdown-preview)). **pi-studio** makes the human an editor.

The **diagram editors bridged to pi** all fail criterion 1. **pi-tldraw** opens a local tldraw MCP canvas where "You can sketch directly in the browser" and `tldraw_canvas_exec` "runs JavaScript against the live editor" ([repo](https://github.com/zeebeeCoder/pi-tldraw)); **pi-tldraw-cloud** needs an OAuth account ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/pi-tldraw-cloud)); **pi-skill-excalidraw**, **pi-excalidraw** and **pi-k-excalidraw** write `.excalidraw` files into a live-synced editor, the last with a screenshot so the model can "visually inspect and self-correct" ([pi-skill-excalidraw](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@ryan_nookpi/pi-skill-excalidraw); [pi-k-excalidraw](https://github.com/kostyay/pi-k-excalidraw)). **DrawMe** is the only pi project with deterministic layout linting, catching "dangling edges, duplicate/reserved ids, malformed geometry, page/container overflow, inadequate margins ... overlaps", but on `.drawio` files, not a live board ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@senad-d/drawme)). **@dev.fast/pi-whiteboard** has no README and describes itself as "Connect Pi before 0.99.0 to dev.fast Whiteboard", a Code-OSS desktop product for code-review visuals whose README names Claude Code and Codex, not pi ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@dev.fast/pi-whiteboard); [devdotfast/whiteboard](https://github.com/devdotfast/whiteboard)). The **terminal image renderers** (`@monotykamary/pi-math` rendering MathJax to Kitty images with "no browser, network request, or child process", the Mermaid-to-ASCII packages, `@mcuste/pi-diagram` for D2, and `@tzssangglass/pi-diagram`, the only one with inline animation) are single-modality and have no board, steps or feedback ([pi-math](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@monotykamary/pi-math); [pi-diagram](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@tzssangglass/pi-diagram)).

The **teaching packages** have no drawing surface. **keating** (36 stars, MPL-2.0, 168 weekly downloads) is the closest pi project to animated lesson visuals: it writes "self-contained Hyperframes animation bundles: function-graphs, distribution-bars, belief-updates, code-traces, timelines, case-diagrams, mind-maps, and concept-cards" under `.keating/outputs/animations/<topic>/`, but these are per-topic file bundles, it needs its own provider credentials, and how bundles are played, whether LaTeX is supported and whether anything is fed back are not stated ([repo](https://github.com/Diogenesoftoronto/keating)). **pi-teach** serves "self-contained HTML documents" from `127.0.0.1` with highlight-to-ask and graded quizzes, with no LaTeX, animation, screenshot or sandbox described ([repo](https://github.com/joshrnoll/pi-teach)). Eero Alvar's **learn** setup (3,910 stars) produces visuals through `svg-maker` and `mermaid-maker` subagents that render to PNG, look at the PNG, and publish a single file into an Obsidian vault note; there is no server, no positioning, no animation, no layout checker, and `write_svg` performs "No sanitization" before the SVG goes to `rsvg-convert` ([SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amosblomqvist/learn/main/skills/visualize/SKILL.md); [svg_tools.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amosblomqvist/learn/main/extensions/visual-tools/tools/svg_tools.ts)). His newer **learnharness** (2,914 stars, AGPL-3.0, Electron plus Next.js on pi's packages) requires a provider API key and exposes no board API; its README mentions no whiteboard, canvas, SVG, Mermaid or KaTeX ([repo](https://github.com/amosblomqvist/learnharness)). **mirador** (21 stars, pushed 2026-10-27) is the one pi host with a strong criterion-8 story, serving "Self-contained HTML pages in a sandbox" on a separate origin with a token cookie and "Tours" that walk through code, but it is a desktop app that hosts pi over ACP and its tours are code walkthroughs, not lessons ([repo](https://github.com/sodre90/mirador)).

Other terminal agents offer no better host. **Claude Code Mods** render `Box, Text, Button, Input, Select, Markdown` and terminal-only `Image`, with `Svg` on "remote surfaces only" and no DOM or HTML, failing criterion 4 ([field guide](https://gist.github.com/ruvnet/a485e930b148185197fc53fd38b429ea)). **claude-tutor** (16 stars) opens a browser "Visual Companion" that renders Mermaid and "step-through visual walkthroughs with CSS transition animations" while the conversation stays in the terminal, but its port, binding, math support, feedback and persistence are undocumented ([repo](https://github.com/JEFF7712/claude-tutor)). **sebi75/agent-canvas** rasterizes agent-written HTML into a terminal image pane ([repo](https://github.com/sebi75/agent-canvas)). Codex's own docs state "Codex CLI and IDE extension: Visualization rendering isn't supported" ([visualizations](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/visualizations)); Gemini CLI has open feature requests for a `visualize` tool and Mermaid preview ([gemini-cli#20393](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/issues/20393)); **Cursor Canvas** (2026-04-15) builds durable React views but is Cursor-only ([blog](https://cursor.com/blog/canvas)). Notebook MCPs such as **datalayer/jupyter-mcp-server** (1,313 stars) return cell images to the agent and autosave, but the human co-edits, cells are linear, and the surface executes arbitrary code, so they fail 1, 5 and 8 ([repo](https://github.com/datalayer/jupyter-mcp-server)).

## Tutoring products copy the chalkboard metaphor, then seal it behind accounts

**Gauth's Unlimited Digital Canvas** validates math-board's spatial design more directly than anything else found. The 1 October 2026 release describes "Chapters expand side-by-side in real time as narration plays", a tutor that "writes its explanation directly onto the board beneath the student's current step", a canvas that "restores a student's exact position and zoom level", and Product Hunt copy adds "you can zoom from the full concept map down to a single formula" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gauth-launches-unlimited-digital-canvas-gives-students-one-continuous-whiteboard-to-visualize-explore-and-understand-complex-topics-302891205.html); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/gauth-ai-course/gauth-unlimited-digital-canvas)). It fails 2 and 3 (consumer app, account, Gauth Plus at "$11.99 per month"), 6 (no external agent) and 8, and whether students can also draw is unstated ([chatgate.ai](https://chatgate.ai/post/gauth-unlimited-digital-canvas)).

The mainstream chat tutors all fail criteria 1, 2 and 3 because they are products, not surfaces. **Khanmigo** gained Gemini-generated interactive diagrams on 2026-08-27 that students manipulate ("dragging a line segment on a graph"), at $4/month with no API ([Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/new-ai-tools-bring-interactive-diagrams-and-targeted-practice-thanks-to-khan-academys-partnership-with-google-org/); [pricing](https://www.khanmigo.ai/pricing)). **Gemini Guided Learning** weaves inline images and videos into responses ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/gemini/new-gemini-tools-students-august-2025/)), and the Gemini app added interactive simulations and 3D models in 2026 ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/3d-models-charts/)). **ChatGPT Study Mode** launched as "custom system instructions" with visuals only "planned", and OpenAI's 2026-03-10 interactive math and science modules cover 70+ topics inside the chat ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/08/study-mode-chatgpt/); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/new-ways-to-learn-math-and-science-in-chatgpt)). **Claude** shipped an interactive-visuals beta on 2026-03-12 "with HTML and XML vector graphics instead of generating images" ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3089660/claude-ai-can-now-explain-concepts-with-interactive-visuals.html)), and Claude Code's Learning output style is text in the terminal ([output styles](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles)). Together these show that every major vendor converged between March and August 2026 on model-drawn vector explanations as a core tutoring feature, inside chat products that a terminal agent cannot address.

The curriculum and institutional products annotate existing widgets or read student work rather than drive a board. **Brilliant's Koji** "can draw on and manipulate the learning environment" but on top of curated interactives, behind Premium ([Brilliant help](https://brilliant.org/help/features/how-does-koji-work/)). **Flint** gives students a shared whiteboard the AI "can view and understand" and "update ... with mathematical graphs", under school accounts ([Flint V5](https://flintk12.com/whats-new/flint-v5)). **Mindjoy** draws on request inline and labels its diagrams "a study aid, not a substitute for the diagram in your textbook" ([Mindjoy docs](https://docs.mindjoy.com/en/articles/14322980-using-the-whiteboard-in-the-student-agent)). **Solversboard** has the student draw and the AI grade, with "Schools supply their own AI provider key" ([solversboard.com](https://solversboard.com/)). **Math Academy** has no LLM tutor but the strongest learner model in the set, a "knowledge graph including levels of mastery" with spaced repetition ([Math Academy](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-it-works)).

The 2025–2026 "AI professor with a whiteboard" startups come closest on criterion 5 and furthest on 2 and 3. **SeeThrough** "builds figures stroke by stroke while explaining it in sync" and uses "a visual router [that] approves or rejects figures instead of guessing" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/1208390)); **MyMagicPencil** claims "90% less bandwidth than video" and exports to video, PDF or slides ([Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/company/mymagicpencil)); **Dudely** "checks that you actually understand before it moves on" ([F4 Fund](https://f4.fund/startups/dudely)); **Penseum**, **LiveClassroom** and **Blackboard.digital** are similar hosted canvases ([Penseum](https://www.producthunt.com/products/penseum/launches/penseum-2); [LiveClassroom](https://www.producthunt.com/p/liveclassroom/liveclassroom-beta-v1); [Blackboard](https://www.producthunt.com/products/blackboard)). **VideoTutor** ($11M seed, Manim-based per its press, "Interrupt anytime. Ask anything.") is a closed cloud product with API requests from 1,000+ organisations but no API ([videotutor.io](https://videotutor.io/); [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/videotutor-11-million-seed-backed-ai-tutoring-platform-surpasses-50-million-tiktok-views/)). **HeyTutor**, now branded Accelute and hosted at app.accelute.co, has the single best verification idea in the survey ("Every diagram is checked before it is drawn ... If a figure cannot be drawn correctly, the tutor teaches without it") and its developer guide confirms "The teaching model cannot draw, label, annotate, erase, or supply diagram coordinates", but it runs on Fireworks Kimi K3 or Azure GPT with Cartesia TTS, has no license file and 1 star ([README](https://github.com/kaizen403/heytutor); [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://github.com/kaizen403/heytutor/blob/main/docs/DEVELOPMENT.md)). None of these exposes an API or local mode.

The math tools with agent hooks are engines, not boards. **Desmos** has shipped no AI features through August 2026 and its API needs a key and CDN script, with self-hosting reserved for partners ([Desmos API](https://www.desmos.com/api/v1.11/docs/index.html)). Community **GeoGebra MCPs** export PNGs the agent can view but inherit GeoGebra's non-commercial terms for installers and web services ([ggb-web-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/kms9/ggb-web-mcp); [GeoGebra license](https://www.geogebra.org/license)). **Wolfram Local MCP** runs with "no cloud roundtrip" but "requires Wolfram|One, Mathematica or a licensed Wolfram Engine" and drives notebooks that execute arbitrary code ([Wolfram](https://www.wolfram.com/artificial-intelligence/mcp/local/)). **Mathigon/Polypad** has no generative-AI feature at all.

## Explainer generators ship video after 9 to 20 minutes of batch rendering

Every Manim-family system outputs a rendered MP4 and must re-render to change anything, which breaks criterion 5 (no mutable scene the viewer is looking at) and criterion 8 (the model writes Python that is executed) regardless of how good the pipeline is. **TheoremExplainAgent** (ACL 2025, MIT) reached a **93.8% render-success rate with o3-mini yet most videos still had layout issues**, needs LiteLLM keys plus Kokoro TTS model files, and has had no README news since June 2025 ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.332); [repo](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/TheoremExplainAgent)). **Code2Video** (ICML 2026, 2,176 stars) has the best feedback loop in the family, a VLM critic reading an occupancy table that traces overlaps, occlusion and unused regions back to source lines, but averages **8.8 minutes per topic with GPT-5 and 15.5 with Gemini 2.5 Pro**, pulls reference images from Google Images and icons from Iconfinder, and its repository license is unstated on the paper page ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174); [repo](https://github.com/showlab/Code2Video)). The 2026 follow-ups are all batch video with frontier-model keys: **LLM2Manim** is explicitly human-in-the-loop with "expert review before the final rendering" and reports a modest 83% vs 78% post-test gain in a 100-student study ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05266)); **OmniManim** adds "structured post-render diagnostics" and layout planning before code ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15585)); **ManimAgent** (EMNLP 2026) scores keyframes with a VLM and keeps a pitfalls memory ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30296)); **PIVOT** adds assessment and remediation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24083)); **MINARD** grounds narration to figure regions ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12576)); **ManiBench** documents 145 ManimGL-to-ManimCE API incompatibilities that models hallucinate ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13251v1)). Several have no public code.

The products wrap the same loop. **Math-To-Manim** exposes its chain over MCP but authenticates with `npx codex login` and warns "Local rendering is not a container security boundary" ([repo](https://github.com/HarleyCoops/Math-To-Manim)). **Animo** is the closest UX, a desktop app with "Realtime Live Preview" that "works on top of your existing subscription" to Claude Code or Codex, but it is closed, costs $49 plus "$35/mo" credits, and exports video ([animo.video](https://animo.video/)). **abhiemj/manim-mcp-server** (655 stars, MIT, stale since 2025-05) has the right integration shape (local MCP, no extra keys) bolted onto the wrong artifact: it "executes Manim animation code and returns the generated video" with no sandbox ([repo](https://github.com/abhiemj/manim-mcp-server)). **manim-web** ports "429 of 429 feasible ManimCE 0.22.0 public symbols" to the browser with KaTeX and claims chemistry and biology modules, but it is a library whose author said "100% was generated by the agent" and whose rendering quality drew complaints; adopting it means executing model-written TypeScript ([repo](https://github.com/maloyan/manim-web); [HN](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/47155375)).

The presentation and animation frameworks are human-authored timelines. **Remotion** is the only vendor-maintained coding-agent integration here, with official skills (`/remotion-studio`, `/remotion-render`, `/remotion-docs`) for Claude Code, Codex, Kimi Code and Cursor, but every change is a React code edit, Studio is editable, and a Company License is required above three employees ([remotion-dev/skills](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills); [LICENSE](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). **Motion Canvas** is reported abandoned since December 2024 with **Canvas Commons** as the community fork, and neither has an LLM integration ([Canvas Commons](https://github.com/canvas-commons/canvas-commons); [HN](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/47155375)). **Manim Slides** steps pre-rendered clips ([repo](https://github.com/jeertmans/manim-slides)); **reveal.js** fragments step DOM elements with no canvas or write-on ([revealjs.com](https://revealjs.com/fragments/)); the **Marp** and **Slidev** MCPs have an LLM write Markdown that a theme lays out ([marp-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@masaki39/marp-mcp/tree/4181ac7d8ea0e1164d8ab941aa51c7e2c81d314f); [slidev-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/kll547n776)). **Google Illuminate** shows only "Experiment" and a sign-in link with no 2026 status ([illuminate.google.com](https://illuminate.google.com/)). Manim proper's only interactive path, `interactive_embed()`, is an IPython embed for the author that the OpenGL FAQ admits is under-documented ([Scene reference](https://docs.manim.community/en/stable/reference/manim.scene.scene.Scene.html)).

## Open-source teacher boards get closest, in the wrong harness or license

**dsh-aha-tutor** is the single nearest match found anywhere and the only project that meets four criteria. Its board fills "in a handwriting font with points, formulas, diagrams, charts and, when it helps, a small animation; each item is written as its sentence is spoken", "curves given by a formula are computed point by point, with sliders", "the lesson's pages sit on one canvas you can drag and zoom while the view follows the teacher", and the viewer can "pause, rewind and change speed any time" ([README](https://github.com/BaronCyrus/dsh-aha-tutor/blob/main/README.en.md)). Its SECURITY.md is the only other document in the survey that enumerates math-board's threat model: "KaTeX runs with `trust: false`", model-drawn SVG is "Parsed and rebuilt through an allow-list of elements and attributes: no scripts, event handlers, `foreignObject`, external `href`", and animations "Run in `<iframe sandbox="allow-scripts">` without same-origin access and with a CSP of `default-src 'none'`" ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/BaronCyrus/dsh-aha-tutor/blob/main/SECURITY.md)). It fails criterion 2 because it is a plugin for the DeepSeek Harness desktop app (installed from the app's Plugins page, using "your DSH default model"), not a pi package or MCP server; it is partial on 1 because the lesson is interactive (quizzes with confidence rating, "asks you to write out your working", raise-hand questions); partial on 4 because no Mermaid, SMILES or illustration library is documented; and unknown on 6 because no layout check or snapshot to the model is described ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/dsh-aha-tutor)). It is MIT, 6 stars, npm 1.9.0, last pushed 2026-10-27.

The other tutor repos are tied to cloud keys and their own chat UIs, so each fails 2 and 3 before anything else is considered. **ChatTutor** (1,315 stars, AGPL-3.0) needs `MODEL_API_KEY` and PostgreSQL and uses a GeoGebra applet with KaTeX ([repo](https://github.com/HugeCatLab/ChatTutor)); its reimplementation **chat-paint** lists "chaotic drawing, overlapping images/text, exceeding the display box" as its main defects ([repo](https://github.com/yongzhuo/chat-paint)). **WhiteBoardAI** (Electron, no license) has the richest surface after dsh-aha-tutor (KaTeX "fractions, integrals, chemical equations", JSXGraph plots, Mermaid, "Custom collision-free placer", history and replay) but generates its teaching script in the cloud via Gemini or Groq ([repo](https://github.com/stefeincurry/WhiteBoardAI)). **ChalkTalk** streams NDJSON steps where "a pen dot follows the drawing, and the camera moves ahead of it", on Gemma 4 via OpenRouter ([dev.to](https://dev.to/danial_mo/chalktalk-a-whiteboard-tutor-that-draws-like-a-teacher-and-lets-you-raise-your-hand-4jmd)). **whiteboard-tutor**, **FeynmanAI**, **Oluko**, **Lumina-AI**, **tutori** and **voice-tutor** are tldraw or Excalidraw canvases driven by Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon Nova, Gemini Live or OpenRouter keys, most with 0–3 stars and several with no license ([whiteboard-tutor](https://github.com/harjothkhara/whiteboard-tutor); [FeynmanAI](https://github.com/Gagancreates/FeynmanAI); [Oluko](https://github.com/Akeem1955/Oluko); [Lumina](https://github.com/shreesha345/Lumina-AI); [tutori](https://github.com/SSHdotCodes/tutori)).

The offline chalkboard generators produce files. **chalkboarding** (24 stars, MIT, built for a NeurIPS 2026 tutorial) is a coding-agent skill that works with "Codex, Kimi Code, OpenCode, Gemini CLI", traces bitmaps instead of freehand, mandates Playwright QA screenshots, and yields "One HTML file, no dependencies" with an eraser button that replays the lesson; it meets 1, 2, 3 and 6 but fails 7 (files only) and 8 (the generated HTML runs scripts) and covers one figure, not a canvas ([repo](https://github.com/lilyzhng/chalkboarding)). **chalk-talk** contributes a negative result from 1,191 Organic Chemistry Tutor videos: board context helps next-word prediction but carrying pen tokens costs the model, and "what to say and what to draw is a language problem; putting it on the board is a rendering problem; the experiment says to keep them apart" ([repo](https://github.com/Tedd618/chalk-talk)). **Atharva chalkboard** runs "layout repair → clamp / de-overlap / (optional) vision fix" before rendering video, at "$0 local" with Ollama and Piper ([repo](https://github.com/Atharva-Kanherkar/chalkboard)). **ai-teaching-board** has no LLM at all but demonstrates a step-zoom camera ("The board zooms into that step so it fills the screen") worth noting as UX ([repo](https://github.com/Tabish810/ai-teaching-board)).

The research prototypes split into two lines, and neither is a learner-facing surface. **DrawDash** (arXiv, December 2025) has the lecturer draw while the AI suggests completions, with "formal classroom evaluation" pending ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01234)); MIT CSAIL and HPI's "whiteboard-literate AI" project (through August 2026) has shipped nothing ([MIT Design](https://design.mit.edu/projects/developing-whiteboard-literate-ai-for-equitable-and-accessible-education)). The "LLM draws to reason" line (**Whiteboard-of-Thought**, whose promised code repo returns 404; **Visual Sketchpad**; **MathCanvas**; **MentalThink**; **SVGLM**) draws for the model's own reasoning, and its 2026 consensus mechanism, "emit SVG, render deterministically, append the image to context, revise", is math-board's `board_update` to `board_snapshot` loop ([WoT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14562); [Sketchpad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09403); [MentalThink](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.03530); [SVGLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30130)). The learner-facing research thread contributes evaluation findings: **DiagramIR**'s rule-based checks on an intermediate representation "agreed more with human ratings than LLM-as-a-Judge" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08283v1)); Logacheva et al. found "LLMs often missed their own mistakes" in educational diagrams even with in-context examples ([arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2601.20476)); **EduIllustrate** reports "sequential anchoring improved Visual Consistency by 13% at 94% lower cost" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05005)).

## Ideas worth borrowing into math-board

The highest-leverage borrowings are prompt and tool-description changes, not renderer changes. From the Manim-agent papers: offer the agent **named anchors** inside each 1200×800 chapter as an alternative to raw coordinates, since Code2Video's ablation found a 6×6 grid beat free placement ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)); attach to each layout warning the **step and item ids that introduced the collision**, mirroring Code2Video's occupancy table; prompt **scope-escalating repair** (fix one item, then one step, then one chapter) after ScopeRefine; have the agent emit **placeholder rects first, run the layout check, then fill**, which is OmniManim's "see before you code" finding ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15585)); run the overlap check **per step, not only on the final chapter**; keep a small **successes/pitfalls file** fed into the tool description, after ManimAgent's dual-channel memory ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30296)); and keep a **per-lesson symbol ledger** in `board_read` so notation does not drift across chapters, after LLM2Manim ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05266)). DiagramIR's finding that rule-based checks beat LLM-as-judge supports keeping the measured-bbox layout report ahead of any vision critique ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08283v1)), and chalk-talk's negative result is direct empirical support for the declarative-items architecture ([repo](https://github.com/Tedd618/chalk-talk)).

From the agent canvases, four feedback-loop ideas stand out. A **Playwright-free snapshot path**, as mcp-excalidraw-server 2.1 did when "Screenshots and image exports no longer need a browser tab" ([repo](https://github.com/yctimlin/mcp_excalidraw)). **Anchored viewer comments or pins** so the learner can point at a board item without breaking view-only, after tldraw's "Agents can't point" post and pi-artifacts' comments-back channel ([tldraw blog](https://tldraw.dev/blog/agents-cant-point); [pi-artifacts](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@nicknisi/pi-artifacts)). **Tiered context** (in-view, focused, peripheral clusters) for `board_read` on long lessons, from the tldraw starter kit ([starter kit](https://tldraw.dev/starter-kits/agent)). A **spec-on-demand tool** (`board_docs`) in the spirit of draw.io's `get_drawio_reference`, Miro's `canvas_get_canvas_composer_skill` and Remotion's `/remotion-docs`, since ManiBench shows API hallucination is the dominant failure in code-shaped scene languages ([drawio-mcp](https://github.com/jgraph/drawio-mcp); [Miro tools](https://developers.miro.com/docs/miro-mcp-tools); [remotion-dev/skills](https://github.com/remotion-dev/skills)). Smaller borrowings: AI-Whiteboard's **free-space map** alongside bounding boxes ([repo](https://github.com/alby13/AI-Whiteboard)); Whimsical's `board_repl`, `undo` and `redo` as agent tools ([Whimsical tools](https://whimsical.com/learn/ai/mcp-tools)); tldraw offline's `server.json` port-and-token discovery and "report `pending` instead of claiming success" apply-status semantics ([third-party skill doc](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/skills/optional/creative/creative-tldraw-offline)); and mermaid-to-excalidraw v3's DOM-free Mermaid layout via Pele if Mermaid is ever measured server-side ([repo](https://github.com/excalidraw/mermaid-to-excalidraw)).

From the tutors, the pedagogy borrowings concern when to draw and how a view-only learner interacts. A **figure router that can decline**: HeyTutor's "If a figure cannot be drawn correctly, the tutor teaches without it" and SeeThrough's router that "approves or rejects figures instead of guessing" ([HeyTutor](https://github.com/kaizen403/heytutor); [SeeThrough](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/1208390)). An explicit **"would a visual help here" decision**, which Khanmigo now makes ("detect the moment when a visual may help a student") ([Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/new-ai-tools-bring-interactive-diagrams-and-targeted-practice-thanks-to-khan-academys-partnership-with-google-org/)). **Ask-at-this-step** with the answer written beneath the paused step, from Gauth ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gauth-launches-unlimited-digital-canvas-gives-students-one-continuous-whiteboard-to-visualize-explore-and-understand-complex-topics-302891205.html)). A **comprehension gate** before the next reveal (Dudely) and a **quiz step whose answer branches into a new chapter** (PIVOT) ([F4 Fund](https://f4.fund/startups/dudely); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24083)). Claude Code's `Insight` blocks and `TODO(human)` slots translate into a board pattern of two why-points after each step and a blank the learner fills before the next reveal ([output styles](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles)). Region-grounded narration (MINARD) argues for pairing each terminal sentence with a camera rect or highlight ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12576)). Export to **PDF or slides** for review appears in Gauth, MyMagicPencil and dsh-aha-tutor and would let the lesson travel while learner state stays local.

From the animation frameworks: a **looping idle step** that repeats its SMIL animation "until the user presses continue" (Manim Slides), **`strike` and `highlight` reveal modes** next to `draw`/`write`/`wipe`/`fade` (reveal.js fragments), **narration-paced reveal** aligning `order`/`duration` to streamed text (manim-voiceover's per-word timing), a **step-zoom camera** option (ai-teaching-board), and the one 3Blue1Brown move the board lacks, a **KaTeX-based equation morph** that manim-web shows is tractable client-side ([manim-slides](https://github.com/jeertmans/manim-slides); [revealjs.com](https://revealjs.com/fragments/); [manim-voiceover](https://voiceover.manim.community/en/stable/); [manim-web](https://github.com/maloyan/manim-web)). On safety, dsh-aha-tutor's `<iframe sandbox="allow-scripts">` with `default-src 'none'` is the technique to adopt if math-board ever wants script-driven animation, and whiteboard-mcp's 0600 token file plus Host/Origin rejection is a tidy reference for the loopback server ([SECURITY.md](https://github.com/BaronCyrus/dsh-aha-tutor/blob/main/SECURITY.md); [security.md](https://github.com/ansuman-shukla/whiteboard-mcp/blob/main/docs/security.md)). On engines, only **JSXGraph** (dual LGPL/MIT, pushed 2026-10-27) and **`@viz-js/viz`** (MIT WASM Graphviz) add capability without license or network risk; Penrose is MIT but heavy, Mafs has had no commit since 2025-03-30, Perseus is "not accepting external contributions", and Desmos and GeoGebra carry key or non-commercial constraints ([JSXGraph license](https://jsxgraph.org/wp/license/); [viz-js](https://github.com/mdaines/viz-js); [Mafs](https://github.com/stevenpetryk/mafs); [Perseus](https://github.com/Khan/perseus/blob/main/README.md)).

## What the researchers could not verify

Several gaps bear directly on the nearest candidates. For **dsh-aha-tutor**, the README does not say whether the board is SVG, HTML or a custom DSL, nor whether any layout check or snapshot reaches the model; the source was not read, so criterion 6 stays unknown. For **agent-whiteboard**, SVG sanitization and compatibility with non-Claude-Code clients are unstated. For **tldraw offline**, no official API reference was reachable; the endpoint list comes from a third-party agent skill doc, and whether a view-only or presentation mode ships in the app versus a community script is unconfirmed. For the official **Excalidraw MCP App**, persistence, screenshots back to the model and rendering outside MCP-Apps hosts are undocumented. For **Gauth**, whether students can draw on the canvas and whether replay exists could not be determined. **sideshow**'s bind address, auth and storage, **keating**'s bundle playback and LaTeX support, **claude-tutor**'s internals, and what **@dev.fast/pi-whiteboard** renders (no README) are all unknown. **whiteboard-mcp.com**'s open-source status and the proprietary **Whiteboard-MCP** binary were not examined.

The searches themselves had limits. pi.dev's gallery search is substring filtering on name, description and author, so a board described with other words could be missed; the Awesome Pi extensions page is 419k characters and only the first 100k were read; several GitHub search queries ("pi-extension canvas", "badlogic pi extension board") were rate-limited and not retried; the pi changelog was read only for 0.99.0 through 1.4.0, so the third-party claim that pi 0.84.0 renders Mermaid in the TUI is unconfirmed. Pages that returned 403 or failed TLS include OpenAI's Study Mode help article (so no evidence either way that its visuals shipped in 2026), PR Newswire in places, npm's page for `@kamiazya/whiteboard-mcp`, learnharness.ai and mymagicpencil.com; the ISTE 2026 launch guide PDF could not be text-extracted, so Gemini-in-Classroom details come from search indexing.

Facts the notes flag as secondary or conflicting: Remotion's skills release date (January 2026) rests on blogs; Motion Canvas's slide mode was not re-verified; the public code status of OmniManim, ManimAgent, PIVOT, Manimator, MINARD, MathCanvas, MentalThink and SVGLM is unconfirmed from abstract pages; Code2Video's repository license is unstated; manim-web's mhchem support and its chemistry and biology modules were not inspected; Google Illuminate's 2026 status and whether Sparks shipped are unknown; VideoTutor's Manim use rests on its own press release; Brilliant Koji's launch date conflicts between June and August 2026; Sizzle's acquirer is unconfirmed; the Desmos API terms are JavaScript-gated; GeoGebra's permission to redistribute its self-hosted bundle offline is unstated; the Thesys C1 to OpenUI relationship was observed only as a redirect; and draw.io's MathJax typesetting and Obsidian's LaTeX rendering inside canvas nodes, both known product features, were not re-verified and would raise those candidates' criterion-4 score to partial. Finally, no peer-reviewed evidence was found that stroke-by-stroke drawing improves learning over static diagrams; the only efficacy numbers in the whole set are Learn Your Way's 60-student RCT (78% vs 67% retention) and LLM2Manim's 83% vs 78% post-test, neither about a live board ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/learn-your-way-reimagining-textbooks-with-generative-ai/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05266)).

## Conclusion

The survey changes the question from "has someone built this" to "why has no one combined these parts", and the answer is that each archetype optimises for something math-board deliberately gives up: the whiteboards for human co-editing, the tutors for voice sync and a bundled model, the generators for a shareable video. The conjunction math-board occupies, a declarative scene the agent cannot script, a lesson timeline the learner can scrub, and a loopback page the learner cannot edit, is not a niche nobody wants; Gauth shipped the same spatial metaphor to consumers three weeks before this report, dsh-aha-tutor converged on the identical sandboxing rules inside a different harness, and every major vendor made model-drawn vector explanations a core tutoring feature in 2026. What is missing from the field is precisely the terminal-agent-addressable, locally rendered version of that idea, which means math-board is currently the only instance of it rather than a late entrant.

Two implications follow. First, the nearest competitors will converge from different directions, and the ones to watch are dsh-aha-tutor (if it decouples from DeepSeek Harness or someone ports its board), agent-whiteboard (if it adds snapshots and a canvas), tldraw offline (if it opens its source or adds a sanitized declarative path), and whatever Gauth exposes next. Second, since the pi ecosystem has an MCP bridge with 1.4M monthly downloads and no MCP teaching board with a feedback loop exists under a permissive license, exposing math-board's five tools over stdio MCP would make it the only such board usable from Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI as well, at the cost of one adapter rather than a redesign.
